The Best Careers on the Board

Twenty-seven seasons, one ruler. LeBron James is so far ahead it’s rude, and one active player is on pace to make it a conversation.

BSBy Ben StupendousOct 7, 20262 min read

Every argument about the greatest careers in basketball eventually turns into an argument about rulers. Rings or stats? Peak or length? Regular season or playoffs? Were the nineties tougher, and does anyone who says so remember what the nineties actually looked like?

We have only one ruler, which makes this easy: wins above replacement, every season since 1999-00, added up.1

LeBron James has 424.2 wins above replacement over 23 seasons. The next-best career that is fully on the board, Chris Paul’s, has 284.8. That gap, 139.4 wins, is by itself a Hall of Fame career. He also leads in career surplus, at $3053M, because for most of 23 years he was worth more than any contract the league allowed him to sign.

Then there are the careers we only see the end of. Dirk Nowitzki, Tim Duncan, Kevin Garnett, Paul Pierce all started before the board does, and still made the top ten on what’s left. Imagine the numbers if we had the start.

The pace

Now the interesting part. Nikola Jokić has 199.9 WAR in 11 seasons, 18.2 a season, the best rate in the top 25 by anyone not named LeBron James. Through his first 11 seasons, LeBron James had 248.9. The master is still ahead, but not by much. Staying there for another decade is, historically, the hard part. Only one man has done it.

The chart is the argument. Every line bends eventually: injuries, age, the slow leak of a first step. The greatest careers aren’t the ones with the highest peaks. They’re the ones whose lines refuse to flatten.
