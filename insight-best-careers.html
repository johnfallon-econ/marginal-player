The Best Careers on the Board

Every season since 1999-00 on one ruler: who added the most, who did it fastest, and the one active player keeping pace with the leader.

By Ben StupendousPosted Oct 5, 2026 · Updated Nov 10, 20264 min read

In February 2023, LeBron James passed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar to become the NBA’s career scoring leader, and the argument about the best career ever got louder instead of settling. Points were never going to settle it. A career is worth what it added to a team, and that’s much harder to count.

People have been trying for a while. Basketball-Reference keeps career leaderboards for Win Shares and for VORP, and LeBron James passed Michael Jordan for the career VORP lead in 2016.1 Ben Taylor ranked the 40 best careers in league history by how much each player raised his teams’ chances of winning a title, adding every season together.2 This is a narrower version of the same question, on our own number.

The rules

One ruler: Marginal WAR (mWAR), the wins a player added above the kind of player any team can sign for the minimum, every season since 1999-00, added up. Beside it, for how good rather than how much, career Marginal Plus Minus (mPM): his rating per 100 possessions, averaged over his seasons by the minutes he played.3

A confession before the list: the board starts in 1999-00, the first season with the play-by-play our ratings need. Anyone whose career began earlier is counted only from there, and they are marked below. Jordan’s Bulls years aren’t here at all. That may not be true for long: the Historical RAPM Project has spent years rebuilding play-by-play for the seasons before it existed, game by game from video. So far it has about one game in six from 1984-85 to 1995-96, and samples of a few games from earlier seasons.4 If it fills in, the career argument gets a lot more interesting. For now, this is a list of the most value added since 1999-00, which rewards being great and being there, not a list of the greatest players.

Worth: each season is worth what the open market paid for that much production that season, as a share of that season’s cap, restated at today’s cap of $155M and added up, so a 2001 season and a 2025 season count alike. Pay doesn’t enter: this is what he was worth, not what he earned.

LeBron James has 419.8 mWAR over 23 seasons. The next-best career that is fully on the board, Chris Paul’s, has 289.8. The gap between them, 130.0 wins, would by itself be the 29th-best career on the board, out of 2,252.

He was also a bargain. He was worth more than he was paid in every one of his 23 seasons, and over his career he returned $2.9B beyond his pay, in today’s money.5

The pace

Nikola Jokić has 201.1 mWAR in eleven seasons: 18.3 a season, against 18.3 for LeBron James. His career mPM, +6.4, is the best of anyone on the board with 20,000 minutes. Through his first eleven seasons, LeBron James had 247.8, so the leader is still ahead at the same point, by 46.7 wins: he started younger.

The rate is the easy part. No one on this board has kept a pace like that for another decade; LeBron James came closest, at 18.3 a season over 23, and Nikola Jokić would need to do it into his forties to catch him.

The one to watch

The one to watch is just starting. Victor Wembanyama has 33.0 mWAR in his first three seasons, and his rating has climbed every year: +3.5 → +4.2 → +5.8. At 22, that is higher than Nikola Jokić was at the same age, +4.4, and level with LeBron James, +5.8.6 The total will take a decade to tell. The rating is the thing to watch each spring.
