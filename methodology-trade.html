Pricing a trade

In broad terms, the aim is to assess the impact of a proposed trade on the aggregate worth of all teams involved. Talents cannot be straightforwardly added and subtracted between teams, given the constraints on each club. Each incumbent is allocated a mPM and hence expected wins and $ equivalent, and the total number of minutes to be apportioned among all players is established for every team involved (sections 1-3). Incumbents are valued over an appropriate span of future time, the change to probability of winning a championship is found (section 4), any picks traded given values according to expected slot and over all years he might be paid as a rookie or on subsequent deals (section 5) and comparable historical outcomes shown (section 6). Apart from the parameters of the championship probability equation (pre-estimated, section 4), all of the following should be amenable to estimation: height and gradient of the curve seating arriving players; proportions applied to the other incumbents and any replacement; height and gradient of picks curve, and deviations of actual picks from expectation; and distribution of outcomes for analogous past trades. The user is allowed to choose the worth of winning a championship ($300M), discount rate on future years’ picks (8%) and number of years over which to estimate wins. All other parameters are arbitrary choices, described at the appropriate point, such as maximum minutes (48; section 1); the respective weights given to number of additional wins and top1 in assessing value of winning (section 4); a number of aspects of the shape of the picks curve (section 5); and minutes for untried rookies (Limitations). Players’ rates are taken as constant with minutes played, for reasons set out in section 2.

1. Total team minutes available

Obviously there are just 240 minutes per game. If incoming players were given their usual minutes, it would be possible to bring in, say, 10 men on around 30 minutes each and include all of these, ie 300 minutes’ worth on a 240-minute team, and a naive trade tool would find it invariably advantageous to bring players in.

Accordingly, all are given anticipated minutes consistent with 240 in total. As this refers to games for his current team, 48 is the upper bound for a man playing a proportion ai of available games:

Σi mi ≤ 240, 0 ≤ mi ≤ 48 · ai

So all players’ minutes are scaled down to total 240 if necessary, with any remainder assumed played by approximately replacement-level players. It makes no difference who fills these and so they are not enumerated, but correspond to 0 additional wins. On formation of each roster, only the best 15 men (measured by WAR) are retained. After any trade all men on the roster are given minutes; if more than 15 remain they are all shown but indicated. As all teams in a trade are valued before and after, all men joining a team must of course deprive current players of some minutes. Method is given in section 3.

This places arrivals better than assuming no change; the predicted changes to everyone else are mostly noise, all valuable. Against logged minutes within seasons since 1999–00, arriving players’ predicted minutes per team-game differed by 6.46 on average, versus 6.77 taking his normal minutes elsewhere (over 1,428 acquired players). For men already on the team, 7.09 versus 7.09 assuming unchanged, over 13,204. Actual changes were smaller than predicted, at 0.58 (se 0.07) per unit predicted change in minutes for acquired players, and 0.21 (se 0.08, r = 0.04) for men remaining. Thus part of the effect of a trade is the incoming player or players and part the adjustments to all other current players. To see how much weight the latter should get, for each of 495 club-seasons 2009–2025 a multiplier was chosen on earlier seasons’ actual changes in wins and used to predict the next. Chosen multipliers were 0.75-1.05. A chosen multiplier is used only if superior to both including in full and excluding altogether, for 90% or more of resamples by season. It was better than excluding altogether for 98% of resamples, and better than in full for 11%, so the changes to other players’ minutes are included in full.

2. Effect of minutes on rate of play

None assumed, but 2 terms arise, both valued at nil.

Extra or reduced rest: rate per minute higher than normal to extent total minutes lower than normal, and lower if higher:

Δrate = 48 · b · [ log(48 − mplayed) − log(48 − mbase) ]

b is set to zero. Over all 754 team-seasons predicted, change in wins was 0.86 times predicted change (regressing on predicted, and controlling for starting distance from 50%), against 0.45 if b=0.04211; mean absolute error by leave-one-season-out was 7.54, against 7.81. This is likely to be important only in distinguishing between differing total minutes.

Further development from increased minutes: no effect on rate of play in subsequent seasons. It cannot be checked against actual values from past trades, which contain no element of it, nor against first season’s wins, as it could only affect later seasons. Estimated as a proportion of actual values from trades, it is −0.68 (se 0.14), the wrong sign.

3. Apportionment of available minutes

New players seated and balance of minutes of those departed allocated among remaining players; if none arrive, a man signed. All parameters but those for the signing estimated using all in-season trades since 1999–00: 966 instances of a club playing at least 5 games before and 5 after the relevant trade, involving 13,916 men. Measured by actual minutes per club game over balance of season for all men concerned, with parameters set to minimize mean absolute difference.

Around 67% (106% for 3 men) of the total minutes of departed men over first 5 games after a trade is played by the new men. All other regulars play on average 3.4 percentage points fewer of total minutes available over first 5 games, rising to 15.3 percentage points fewer over games 21-40.

Each arriving player’s share of 240 is

sharenew = −0.0351 + 1.02 · sold + 0.0760 · (hole / 240) + 0.0121 · (W / 41)

where sold is expected share if no trade, hole the minutes per game vacated by all sold, W predicted wins. So roughly his existing load minus around 6 minutes a game (for a 41-win team) plus around 8% of those vacated.

These are allocated pro-rata to current players’ s-0.141·(h-s)-0.621, s being current share and h (38/240) the share at which headroom, h-s, is floored at 0.002. So minimum weight around 7 minutes a game, increasing as a function of minutes above that, and 1.9 times as much for 30 as 10 minutes. None allocated above 48 · a. To extent total allocated to new men exceeds total minutes of men sold, this reduces all current players’ total minutes in proportion to their shares. For no men received, a signed player at 17.8 + 0.0670 · hole - 0.179 · W minutes per game. These last 3 parameters are not re-estimated, since only the no-arrival trades identify them.

By leave-one-out by season: mean absolute difference of 6.94 minutes per player per game, compared to 7.05 for previous parameters and 6.97 for current players continuing and arrivals playing normal load. Better than previous parameters 26 out of 27 times and than no change 18 times.

A separate set of parameters is used for trades prior to start of season, similarly estimated from all 1,539 instances of clubs trading before opening night (20,622 men) and measured over full season:

sharenew = −0.00336 + 1.03 · sold + 0.0324 · (hole / 240) − 0.0140 · (W / 41)

and capacity exponents of -0.186 and -0.782. So an arrival keeps around current load, less about 4 minutes per game at 41 wins. These parameters apply over all full seasons played after an offseason trade (that is, current season with “Trade made: today” selected, and all subsequent seasons), while the others apply only until end of current season following an in-season trade. Leave-one-out: 7.085 minutes, 7.093 applying the other set and 7.088 no change, so superior to in-season parameters 20 of 27 times. However, in terms of predicting club wins, applying these sets as appropriate gives mean absolute error over 757 club-seasons of first season after trade of 7.468, versus 7.444 applying the in-season set to all seasons (bootstrapping by season: 90% CI for difference −0.0003 to +0.0468).

Prediction of actual minutes: all considered here are applied to 80 club-sides left out of estimation, that is 30% at random of the 270 in-season club-sides since 2018–19, and the arrival parameter or parameters re-estimated. Mean absolute errors of predicted from actual minutes per man per game are quoted. All men who might reasonably fall within scope of prediction under the relevant method, that is, currently playing and going on to play, and those acquired, are considered. Other men playing that season for the relevant club are excluded from all calculations. Clearly an error of about 6 minutes is very sizeable relative to typical values in the region of 15-30. Much of the benefit of the final method relative to simply spreading all minutes among the men continuing seems to be from predicting minutes for arriving player or players: identifying them, with no other information, gives 6.03. Methods to further distribute among current players make little difference. On shares only, distributing according to similarity to man or men departing (the kernel, whose resemblance measure explains player-game minutes within a player-season at R² 0.0302 against 0.0256 for position, on 77,156 player-games) gives 5.96, marginally better than 6.00 for the final method, by only 0.042 minutes over 80 club-sides. However allowing for error in total minutes, 6.42 against 6.35, the kernel predicting 6.78 more minutes in total. All methods with an overall level predict too many minutes for men considered; 23.4 too many per game under the final method, unavoidably to some degree since other men not considered will be playing. It would follow that any method spreading all minutes among these men would over-predict to this extent.

Worst of all methods, save removing the departed and renormalizing, since there is no way of increasing any man to make up for another. Predicting log of ratio of man’s minutes to overall average means no overall expected level of minutes can be incorporated, and in fact all a team’s men sum to 1.0 for each game. Hence no figure can be given for overall level.

These are purely with reference to prediction of minutes, with no implication as to effect on values.

4. Probability of winning championship

It seems reasonable to suppose teams seek to maximise probability of winning a championship rather than simply expect wins. This is modelled as logistic in expected wins and best current player (top1), with expected value taken over all possible actual wins, and each club’s share taken as proportion of total over all clubs:

Pt = Ew[ σ( β0 + β1 · w + β2 · top1t ) ], w ~ N(Wt, sdt)
sharet = Pt / Σt′ Pt′, equityt = V · (sharetafter − sharetbefore)
β = (−16.4, 0.217, 0.118)

These were estimated by unpenalized ML for all club seasons 1996–97 to 2025–26 with a known winner (892, over 30 years and 30 winners). Whether won or not is regressed on regular season wins (all scaled to 82 games, to match the 82-game projections the tool supplies) and top1 mWAR. No other covariates. Thus each unit mWAR of best player increases log odds of winning by 0.118, se 0.047, z 2.5. Omitting gives LR statistic 6.3 on 1 df. sdt is stdev of possible wins, floored at 6. Wt after trade is moved by 80% of predicted change to first season wins from the trade. top1 is the greater of 0.85 times incumbent top1 and best man after reallocation. V, worth of winning a championship, can be set from $100M to $600M, default $300M.

As all 30 clubs are in the denominator, clearly all others lose to extent any one or more increase. Hence all clubs affected by reallocation from a trade are recalculated, with the remainder pro-rata. There is no reason why predicted wins should sum appropriately, since each club is considered playing “average” opposition; hence all increased or decreased equally to give 1,230.

It follows from the non-linearity, exacerbated by top1 term, that bringing more good players together will tend to be rewarded more than proportionately. No attempt has been made to determine if this is reasonable (see Limitations).

5. Value of picks

Since unknown who will actually be selected, a pick should be thought of as giving the right to select from a distribution. Its worth derives from cheapness of rookie deals relative to likely output, not because rookies are good.

surplus(s) = v̄(s) − rookie cost(s)
pick value = (capy / cap2026–27) · Σs P(slot = s) · surplus*(s) / (1 + r)t

where v̄(s) is smoothed expected value at slot s to the drafting club; surplus*(s) is surplus(s) unless s is protected, when it is the value passed on to other party; r rate of reducing value with years to run, default 8%, and t years to the draft of season y.

As drafted, the player the pick will become. All possible selections valued conditional on being drafted by that club, that is value during his rookie deal and any excess over pay on later deals, pay being the maximum salary or what comparable players re-sign for (including any declining contracts). By methods set out under Drafts, this gives an expected value for each slot, which is then smoothed allowing increasing numbers of surrounding slots as further away:

v̂(s) = α̂s where (α̂s, β̂s) = argmin Σi K( (pi − s) / h(s) ) · [ vi − α − β(pi − s) ]²
h(s) = 2.0 · (1 + 0.06 · (s − 1)), K Gaussian

Over whole career. Again values from board, conditional on club. This will not be varied with choice of number of years to assess current players. These will be over 2, 3 or 4 seasons or to age 40 inclusive (on existing deal and any assumed renewals), default the latter. Hence with default a pick and player are considered over the same period; with shorter periods, longer for the eventual pick.

In addition, current players would be valued at the expected cap in each season (from $165M 2026–27 rising to $1,046M 2050–51), undiscounted. Hence even on default periods, a pick differs in that he is valued with reference to the cap for his draft year in all subsequent years of his career, discounted back at rate r, so that later years are assessed at the earlier, lower cap.

Currency. All expressed in equivalent terms. That is, expected values over whole career expressed at a single point in time, to correspond to board’s assessment. So all years for men drafted in a particular year expressed in terms of cap for first year of their rookie deals. On 2026-27 cap of $165M this gives surpluses of $262M, $226M, $107M, $48M and $18M at slots 1, 5, 14, 30, 45. These are of course scaled to relevant year and discounted in assessing a particular pick.

Calibration. Expected values delivered by actual selections can be measured, and so the curve adjusted accordingly. For each slot the average actual value delivered over classes 2007–2016 relative to board assessment, both averaged over slots in the same manner as above, gives a multiplicative factor. These are limited to [0.3, 1.5] and similarly smoothed over slots, running 0.30 to 1.08; approximately 10 classes fall into each. So over first 40 picks vary only between 0.84-1.08, 0.84, 1.08 and 0.97 for slots 1, 5, 14 respectively, then steadily falling to 0.84, 0.66 and 0.30 by slots 45, 50, 60. So considerable over-estimation of later second round picks. An isotonic regression is then applied, so value never increases with slot.

Of course, once costs of rookies deducted, this need not continue to decline. In particular, slot 2 gives a slightly higher surplus, $268M, than the 1st, $262M, as the multiplier is 0.84 at the 1st and 0.94 at slot 2 but expected value declines by only $1M, compared to $7M lower cost. Also slots 30 and 31 give $48M to $51M due to large fall in cost entering second round.

Reason for prior smoothing. There being about 20 observations per slot, one exceptionally successful (or unsuccessful) player makes a large difference, and indeed the unsmoothed curve increases from one slot to the next in 23 instances out of 59. Isotonic regression would then combine a number of adjacent slots, so giving constant expected value and tending to increase surplus (given falling costs). This tending to combine many slots is reduced by prior smoothing.

Reason for local linear. It can be seen that any local method at the first slot would include only slots giving lower values. Estimating and applying slope therefore seems desirable at least in this region.

Increasing span. Steepness of curve over early slots would argue against wide smoothing, while very little variation over second round suggests wide span appropriate. So spans equivalent to about 80 men for first slot, 165 for fifth, 252 for 14th, and 324-497 over second round.

Range of probable slots. As described under Drafts, number of other teams predicted to lose more games, allowing ±6 around their prediction for next season, and ±√(6² + 3.5²·(y−1)) for y seasons time. Probabilities then according to official league schedule for relevant year, with second round picks starting at 30 plus team’s position. A pick falling within a protection does not transfer, and is valued as equivalent offer next year under applicable rules, or other stated pick, or nil. Any option to receive further pick should original prove superior valued jointly over same set of random draws, with protected case receiving better pick. If multiple descriptions of provisions given, assumed unprotected, value capped accordingly, and indicated. Finally, second round surpluses truncated at nil (or $0.01M practically), so some value to protecting these, as 5 of 30 slots sit at that floor. Obviously no negative values, given possibility of waiving player on minimum salary; a pick is an option, not a debt.

6. Uncertainty

All values predicted are ex-ante and on the assumption of no aversion to risk. Hence equal expected values imply equal desirability whatever variability. Uncertainty enters (i) in averaging over range of probable wins to give probability of winning; (ii) in range of slots for any given pick; and (iii) ranges of values from past similar trades are shown. However, no adjustment has been made to values assigned to individual likely picks to allow for risk aversion (constant relative risk aversion).

Interpretation of a Process Grade. Output includes range of values delivered by similar trades. All team-sides to trades over 1999–2025 (2,763) are considered, with more recent ones extended for the duration of outstanding contracts based on current predictions. Various quantiles found by regression on the process grade and overall magnitude of the trade. Consistent with the empirical evidence, spread of outcomes increases with size. Fitting on preceding years only and testing on the next, 80% interval contains 78% of actuals and 50% interval 48%. Over varying sizes of trade: 78%, 77%, 82%, 78%. Middle outcome moves about 76 cents per dollar of process.

Limitations

  • Possible excessive value on very good combinations of players. As described above, highly non-linear in total quality of squad, with additional specific term for best man. No check of validity of this prediction.
  • Assumes no dislike of risk. Hence equal expectation sufficient to equate items, whatever variability. Some front offices may be unable to build and operate multiple squads, and so place greater weight on certainty.
  • No allowance for other characteristics of players. Fit with others on team, coach, wishes re continuing all ignored.
  • Feasibility of trades ignored. Various rules regarding players’ salaries ignored.
  • Does not assume optimal utilisation of players. Playing time simply follows historical patterns, so a poorly-managed team is valued accordingly.
  • Difficult to interpret allocation of minutes. Simply apportioning all minutes among remaining current players only gives 0.62 min. worse per player. Also clearly some minutes go to others no rule could have identified on the day.
  • Rookies with no playing time given fixed amount by slot only. All such players (for example any current season draft man, picks 1–30) would be assumed to play 72% of games for 28 minutes if picked in the top 5, 22 if 6th-14th, and 16 thereafter. Their rate of play then adjusted appropriately to give the total assessed by the board for first season. So no variation according to specific slot within these bands. No allowance made for untried second round picks and free agents.
  • Subject to assumptions underlying value of current players (Value Board; see here) and likely draft picks (draft board; see here).
