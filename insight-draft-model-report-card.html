The Draft Model’s Report Card

Fifteen drafts, graded on the calls that cost real money — the stars that it got right, the ones that it got wrong — and what it makes of the 2026 class.

By Ben StupendousPosted Sep 25, 2026 · Updated Nov 26, 202610 min read

4.2 million viewers tuned in to see the results of the NBA Draft in June of last year,1 and 2.3 million to the Draft Lottery six weeks prior, whose sole purpose is to determine team draft position.2 Players in the opening 4 years of their contract have accounted for ~26% of league mWAR (Marginal Wins Above Replacement) and ~16% of salary over the previous nineteen years. Of course there will be extreme skew within a class, with the top 3 returning ~44% of a typical class’ aggregate value.34

There’s no shortage of examples of failed draft picks. The substack Freezing Cold Takes focuses exclusively on these. But to my knowledge no one has publicly subjected an equivalent board to similar scrutiny. Obviously there are many mock drafts available online, but few explain their underlying process. Those that were openly accessible appear to have been absorbed by front offices: John Hollinger’s Draft Rater ceased publication when the Memphis Grizzlies signed him in 2012, Layne Vashro’s draft projection calculator ended when Vashro joined the parent firm of the Denver Nuggets, and Kevin Pelton left ESPN in March to join the Houston Comets. Only the author of ESPN’s RPM, Jeremias Engelmann, took the opposite path, having worked for the Phoenix Suns and Dallas Mavericks before publishing his draft model again. Front offices must believe there’s value to be gained.

The rules

This line of thinking originates with Cade Massey and Richard Thaler’s concept of ‘surplus’. A player’s surplus is the difference between his implied value and what he costs. Using the NFL as a proxy, they showed that the leading draft picks were heavily overpaid relative to their contribution, while the final selection in Round 1 generated greater surplus than the #1 pick.5 Applying the logic to the NBA, Arturo Galletti and Nate Silver extended the analysis through the end of the player’s rookie contract.6 The leading 3 picks generated ~three times the surplus generated by picks 23-30.7 Once subsequent years are included, this roughly doubles for any adjacent bracket of picks.8

The record below comes from a recipe I chose knowing how all fifteen classes turned out.910 11 For the current draft, the leading 10 prospects have an expected value ~five times their middle estimate, as shown in the subsequent tables. And 69% of that is achieved post-rookie deal.

The grade

Each player was paired with another from the same class, scaled to the difference in value between the 2.12 The board was correct in 84% of the $ spent, while the draft was 78%. Furthermore, the board outperformed the draft in fourteen of the fifteen classes, greater than we’d expect by chance. Excluding the inclusion of Jokić in the draft, the board was superior in thirteen of fourteen.13

If we treat each player equally, by rank rather than by the dollars at stake, we get a much weaker result: the board outperformed the draft in eight of fifteen classes, an average margin luck alone would match 60% of the time.14 As discussed, the vast majority of picks in any given class fail to generate significant value, and the board is no better than the 30 front offices on those.

In 2014 forty players went before Nikola Jokić. The board assigned Jokić 4th, despite projecting a middle outcome of losing $37M. Its best single guess was that he would never be worth what a club pays a pick that high, but it took the chance that a 19-year-old center in the Serbian league became a star. Since then he’s been worth $1.6B to Denver, vs a middle of −$20M for George Hill and −$21M for Jimmy Butler.

Derrick White also features: drafted at 29th, assigned position 13th by the board and placed 4th in his class. To date he has returned $532M to his teams, $376M of which have accrued under deals signed since the conclusion of his rookie contract.15 Boston has been collecting since February 2022.

The stars it didn’t

Of the 45 players who finished in the top three of their class, the board had 31 in its top ten and ranked 30 higher than the teams did and 10 lower. Including: Russell Westbrook, drafted fourth in 2008, 15th on the board and assigned an expected value of $53M, who has returned $886M; and Joel Embiid, taken third in 2014 while the board had him at 9th.

Of the board’s own top 3 picks in each class, 35 of 45 finished in the top ten of their class. Of the actual 1st, 2nd and 3rd overall selections: 22.16 The board’s top-three picks that didn’t:

Christian Anderson Jr., selected eighteenth, is sixth on the board, with Expected and Middle outcomes of $289M and $0.1M respectively. This represents the largest ratio of Expected to Middle in the board’s top 10.

Maliq Brown went 44th and the board has him 15th on his ‘motor’ and ‘feel’, its biggest bargain. However, it attaches minimal importance to his projected shooting, where the scouts’ grade places him towards the bottom of the scale.18

After five seasons I’ll grade this class the same way. In the meantime, I’ll look to see if Christian Anderson Jr. (eighteenth) achieves his upper bound and Maliq Brown develops as a shooter.
